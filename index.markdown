<h1>Silicon Chip Implant Marvel</h1>
<p>Members of the Indiana School of Medicine led by Chandan Shen have developed a nanochip device that can change skin cells into nerve and blood cells, which could revolutionize the future of modern medicine. "This small silicon chip enables nanotechnology that can change the function of living body parts," says team leader Chandan Shen. "For example, if someone's blood vessels were damaged because of a traffic accident and they need blood supply, we can't rely on the pre-existing blood vessel anymore because that is crushed, but we can convert the skin tissue into blood vessels and rescue the limb at risk."</p>
<p>The nano chip is made of silicon. It is equipped with millions of nanometers  that suck up cells to the main body of the chip, whiere it reprograms them to fit specific genes like brain cells and blood cells.</p>
</article>
<article>
<h1>Legal Implications, IU School Of Medicine </h1>
<p>However, due to the fact that the chip might increase the rates of cancerous cells, it must be thoroughly tested and improved by the team. After it is proven to be safe enough for  actual patients, it will have to be sent to the FDA for approval. Once it receives approval, it might be able to replace brain cells damaged by a stroke or repair nerve damage as a result of diabetes. It will also help add blood cells to the blood stream if bone marrow production isn't working properly.</p>
